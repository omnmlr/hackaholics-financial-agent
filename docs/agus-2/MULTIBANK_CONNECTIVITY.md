# AGUS 2.0 — multi-bank connectivity decision

9 November 2026. Status: **research complete for architecture review; banking launch blocked on institutional enablement and coverage/price evidence**. This does not authorize AGUS development, deployment or transactions.

## Verdict

The strongest discovered route is an **institution-sponsored, bank-originated personal-account initiation channel**, with AGUS providing conversation, memory and reviewed instructions, while an authorized institution controls customer/account authorization and executes a single bank-to-bank payment. **Prioritize partner access to Remita's existing consumer account-profiling/payment product**, rather than generic corporate RITS credentials: current first-party materials advertise profiled-account balances and document user-approved transfers. This is a concrete priority lead, not a selected/available API. Use open-banking account/consent/payment contracts where actually supported; a bank-sponsored standard-gateway pilot is the alternative if an existing consumer product cannot be delegated. Do not collect into an AGUS merchant account and then fund an unrelated payout.

This is a concrete route to validate with a partner, **not a presently available 20-bank integration**. No inspected public product/code/standard establishes all of: 20 existing Nigerian personal source banks, balances, general history, arbitrary authorized beneficiary payments, acceptable consumer delegation and equivalent bank charges. We did not independently connect or pay from any bank. It would be equally unsupported to call the requirements impossible or to call sponsorship alone a solved architecture.

The useful change from the earlier feasibility report is that bank-side reusable implementation and institutional account-originated mechanisms were found and inspected. AGUS need not invent an exchange, replace users' accounts or build an entire payment network. The first dependency is now a specific **consumer-source operational role and interface**, not “find some API keys.”

## Proposed mechanics and authority boundaries

Keep the accepted recovered AGUS application as the conversational product. The research does not select a new application framework or reconstruct recovery work. Add a financial-provider boundary only after the institutional model is accepted.

1. **Register and verify eligibility.** AGUS authenticates its own user. The authorized participant completes the required customer due diligence and binds a verified identity to the source account. Agree how verified 18+ eligibility is established; self-entered DOB is insufficient. Prefer a minimized signed age/identity assertion from an approved onboarding authority rather than AGUS collecting BVN/NIN documents or bank credentials. This assertion is a proposed contract, not a current provider feature.
2. **Discover real capabilities.** A partner supplies an authenticated, versioned list of source institutions and operations for the approved customer/account type: linking, balances, history, payment initiation, revocation, limits and fees. Public directories and recipient lists cannot populate accepted coverage.
3. **Link through the institution.** Open its approved bank/participant-hosted consent flow. The user authenticates and selects the existing account and read/payment permissions. Code/auth responses are bound to the initiating user/session, registered client, redirect, requested scopes and issuer. Use that partner's approved OAuth/OIDC/FAPI/mTLS profile where applicable; do not invent an incompatible token convention.
4. **Keep authority with its issuer.** Bank credentials stay with the bank. The participant retains any credentials that only it may use; AGUS receives only an explicitly permitted scoped token or projection. Calling an outsourced service “bank-hosted” cannot legalize unapproved AGUS data access. Separate read consent, payment consent, AGUS approval and bank strong authentication.
5. **Read and remember facts.** Normalize actual available balances and paginated history with source time, coverage, currency and provenance. AGUS memories refer to authorized records and invalidate access on revocation. Observed sends/SMS cannot substitute for complete bank history.
6. **Prepare the instruction.** “Send N20,000 to Chidera” resolves the correct person/account through approved beneficiary resolution; ambiguity causes clarification. The model creates an untrusted proposal. Server code computes integer-kobo values and binds source account, verified beneficiary, amount, bank/route, charge ceiling, expiry and one-use reference to the user's review.
7. **Authorize at the source.** The participant/bank must accept that exact beneficiary payment under its approved consumer flow. The user completes required bank SCA/OTP/app confirmation. AGUS cannot satisfy this with its own password or merchant credentials. The API's precise order of payment-consent creation, approval and execution governs; an approved amount must not silently become a broad reusable debit grant.
8. **Execute one account-originated payment.** The authorized institution invokes its permitted source route directly to the beneficiary. Keep funds outside an AGUS collection pool. A sponsor may use a scheme/processor only if it has the necessary authority for that source bank and customer. If the only available route opens a new wallet or collects to AGUS first, it does not satisfy this selected design.
9. **Reconcile independently.** Persist an execution claim/reference before the side effect. Distinguish submitted, bank-authentication-pending, processing, source debit observed, provider-confirmed success, recipient credit evidenced, failed, reversed and unknown. A returned app callback or signed webhook is a trigger for verification, not sufficient proof of recipient credit. Do not resend ambiguous payments; reconcile the original reference.

AGUS owns intent, scoped responsibilities, policy, proposal/review and evidence presentation. The authorized participant owns the agreed financial service, source authority, regulated execution, bank/provider integration and allocated compliance/complaints duties. Banks own customer accounts, bank authentication and their core ledger. Contracts must assign responsibilities; a label or diagram cannot do so.

The OBN gateway is a **reference to evaluate**, not a mandated deployment. Its full dependency stack and GPL obligations may be unsuitable for a small pilot; the sponsor's existing gateway could expose the same approved contract. The seven tests do not certify FAPI, token revocation, mTLS, gateway availability or upstream bank security.

## Reaching 20 source banks

There are only two evidenced mechanism shapes to pursue: an authorized intermediary already offering the approved personal-account service across those banks, or bank-by-bank participation behind common contracts. A single sponsor plus common software does not automatically eliminate the latter work. Require actual upstream source coverage before estimating that one adapter reaches 20 banks.

[SOURCE_BANK_ACCEPTANCE.csv](research/SOURCE_BANK_ACCEPTANCE.csv) records a proposed 20-institution validation roster. It is **not supported-bank marketing**: every row starts unestablished and unaccepted. Include deposit banks and selected microfinance banks because existing digital-bank accounts matter; names/types/current legal status and final prioritization must be confirmed before qualification. Do not substitute payment wallets or count multiple channels of the same bank twice.

Each accepted row needs all of:

- The exact existing **personal account type**, institution/issuer and permitted AGUS/participant role; an approved funding source, not merely a receiving bank.
- Bank-approved customer linking, ownership binding, read consent and revocation; actual balance and history evidence with coverage/freshness recorded.
- A permitted source debit/credit-initiation flow to the selected personal beneficiary, SCA and supported account limits. A merchant mandate cannot silently become general P2P authority.
- Sandbox/contract checks followed, under **separate future authorization**, by real customer-consented reads and a controlled payment with independently checked amount/recipient/status and recipient credit. Test-only claims stay separate.
- Same-source ordinary-transfer comparison, complete charges and who funds each additional API/operations cost, plus incident/reversal/support ownership.

None of those future live operations is authorized by this research. One successful pilot qualifies one route, not twenty. Until 20 distinct records pass, the founder's intended banking launch remains blocked; statement upload or a manual bank-app handoff is not relabelled as launch completion.

## Costs and smallest blockers

AGUS can set its own markup to zero. The unresolved economic condition is that the **user's total route charges must not exceed that bank's equivalent ordinary transfer**, including free-transfer entitlements, taxes and any new channel fee. A collection-plus-payout route, USSD session charge, card acquiring or FX leg cannot be hidden by calling it a normal bank fee. [Cost evidence and comparison rule](research/PROVIDER_AND_COST_AUDIT.md).

A plausible funding mechanism is a bank treating AGUS as its own customer acquisition/service channel, retaining its usual transfer economics and explicitly funding the added integration/operation costs. **No institution has committed to that arrangement.** No free production API, supplier assurance, KYC, infrastructure or ongoing subsidy was established. The development budget stays N0: no paid signup or live trial was attempted.

The **first smallest unresolved dependency** is one named, appropriately authorized institution accepting the **consumer-delegated existing-source-account role** and supplying the corresponding account/consent/payment/status interface. This is more specific than a generic sponsorship letter, merchant account or API key. The next independent blockers are 20-bank source/read coverage and a commercially funded fee-parity model; resolving the first does not resolve the other two.

The concrete next action is to submit the [prepared partner validation brief](research/PARTNER_VALIDATION_BRIEF.md) first to Remita's appropriate **consumer-channel/product partnership** team. It asks whether its actual personal-account model can be exposed under an authorized AGUS partner arrangement, with actual source/read/payment and fee evidence. **It has not been sent.** If it cannot be delegated, the next route is an anchor bank's sponsored standard-gateway pilot. If either offers only its own source bank, corporate payroll, merchant collection or a bank-code list, record that limitation; do not begin coding against a fiction.

## Alternatives and experiment limits

The four research reports—[open banking](research/OPEN_BANKING_PROTOCOLS.md), [networks/Remita](research/NETWORK_SPONSORSHIP.md), [device/protocols](research/DEVICE_AND_OPEN_SOURCE.md) and [providers/costs](research/PROVIDER_AND_COST_AUDIT.md)—preserve the serious alternatives, pinned repositories, official evidence, licensing and exact failures. They cover national rails/direct debit, RITS, Interswitch/PayAttitude, alternative unified APIs, app intents/USSD, UK/Brazil consent protocols, FinTS/EBICS and collection/card/wallet routes. No new blockchain experiment was justified: a chain cannot grant authority over a bank account. Solana USDC and USD receiving/conversion remain **committed separate capabilities**, with their earlier integration gates unchanged.

Practical result: the unchanged OBN handler passed seven local authorization checks; the offline Remita wire-contract audit reproduced its preserved output; source/endpoint audits exposed coverage and permission gaps. None proves customer onboarding, bank connection, a valid financial grant, real balance/history, execution, fee parity or 20-bank readiness. No full banking PoC could honestly be executed without the missing access and separate financial authorization. Research milestones and scripts are saved on the isolated GitHub research branch; application code, recovery and browser execution remain untouched.
