# Business model and pricing hypotheses

**Charge for successfully reconciled invoices and downloadable evidence bundles.** Keep basic invoice creation, direct-to-wallet settlement and a small number of verified receipts free. Let the freelancer choose premium bookkeeping evidence at an illustrative $1 per reconciled invoice or a prepaid pack, charged separately from principal. **Use metered business automation as the secondary revenue model.** Include an audit log, approvals and exports. Charge per successful operation (illustrative $0.30) and offer prepaid credits for teams that do recurring volume.

Base the proposition on work saved during collections and close. Existing direct-wallet transfers should remain usable without charging a percentage or requiring an upgrade. Bill only for a delivered reconciliation result or a verified business action. Show all network fees and amount to the recipient in the review. Billing that adds a second on-chain transfer to each small payment may be worse than aggregated billing.

## Illustrative monthly unit economics

These are separate planning scenarios, mostly at pilot scale. Volumes are assumptions and rows should not be added. Contribution margin here means (revenue minus attributable delivery cost) / revenue. Direct cost estimates include metered infrastructure and support allocations. They exclude fixed product engineering, customer acquisition, corporate tax and a future compliance team. No conversion or price is validated.

| Model and payer | Illustrative usage and net revenue | Attributable cost and margin | Adoption risk and dependencies |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Merchant collection fee, paid by merchant | $50,000 collected × 0.35% = **$175** | $105 RPC, checkout and support → **40%** | MoonPay Commerce 1–2% and Stripe listed rates include broader services. A fee on principal gives users a reason to bypass AGUS for large invoices. A routed/split payment and jurisdiction review add complexity. |
| **Reconciled invoice and proof, paid by freelancer** | 120 paying users × 8 paid matches × $1 = **$960** | $49 indexed RPC + $45 worker/DB + $0.07 × 960 = $161.20 → **83%** | Request Finance offers free invoices. Charge for time saved, reference match, usable export and exception handling. Price sensitivity and manual match cost are the main unknowns. Keep the payment direct to the recipient. |
| Eligible swaps, paid by trader | $100,000 volume × 10 bps = $100, less 20% Jupiter share = **$80** | $50 quotes, integration support and monitoring → **38%** on net | Public user price comparison is easy. Require explicit fee/minimum-out disclosure, supported referral setup, rate/mint monitoring and legal review. No incentive to recommend a trade solely to charge a fee. Defer. |
| Metered agentic execution, paid by user | 4,000 accepted actions × $0.06 = **$240** | $130 worker, model, receipts, limited fee subsidies → **46%** | Bankr benchmarks above. Near-free transfer alternatives make low-value executions difficult to monetize. Costs rise with failed attempts and long AI context. Requires reliable authorization and worker isolation. |
| **Business payment automation, paid by a studio/team** | 10 teams × 250 completed operations × $0.30 = **$750** | $250 scheduling, logs, support and compliance allocation → **67%** | Benchmark Request Finance $300 plan includes workflows/reconciliation. Revenue needs durable approval records, service levels and reconciliation. Team support and custody/compliance can dominate costs. Start with capped pilot usage and no principal custody. |
| APIs/integrations, paid by a platform | 100,000 successful calls × $0.003 = **$300** | $140 indexed reads, signed webhook delivery, tenancy and support → **53%** | Generic Helius data costs $49 for 10m credits. Sell verified invoice/settlement records and delivery guarantees. Requires authenticated customers, tenant isolation and a defensible SLA. |
| On/off-ramp referrals, paid by partner | **$0 committed**. Sensitivity only, $20,000 settled eligible volume × hypothetical 0.30% = $60 | $30 partner integration/support → **50% conditional**, before minimums or clawbacks | MoonPay/Coinbase distribution is geography, approvals and KYC dependent. End-user charges are not AGUS revenue. Require a signed commercial schedule and disclosure. A poorer rate could erode trust. [MoonPay on-ramp](https://dev.moonpay.com/widget/on-ramp/overview) · [MoonPay fee disclosure](https://www.moonpay.com/legal/pricing_disclosure) |
| Export/review pack, paid by freelancer or bookkeeper | 50 period packs × $5 = **$250** | $55 job, storage, review and support → **78%** | Free CSV is common. The paid product must provide an attributable history, reconciliation evidence and useful exceptions. Jurisdiction-specific tax claims require specialist review. |

At a planning SOL price of $150, the documented 5,000-lamport base fee is $0.00075 per signature, or $0.0015 for two. Priority fees, ATA/rent deposits, RPC backfill, retries and sponsored failed transactions change the result. Direct invoice transfers can have the payer pay network fees. For the invoice model, $0.07 variable assumes $0.02 indexed reads/storage and bounded inference plus $0.05 support per reconciled payment. With variable cost rising to $0.20, gross contribution on the same cohort drops to 70% before acquisition. At only 30 payers and eight receipts each, the base $94 allocation and $0.07 variable cost leave about 54%. Measure repeat payments, manual exceptions, average support time, RPC credits per invoice, paid conversion and avoided reconciliation time before raising spend. [Solana fees](https://solana.com/docs/core/fees) · [Helius billing](https://www.helius.dev/docs/billing/plans)

For commercial launch, get jurisdiction-specific advice on transfers, custody, fees, records, tax exports and sanctions. Hosted signer, conversion or payouts may change the obligations. Partner-controlled ramps should handle applicable identity checks and return a capability or quote before any promise. Keep invoice metadata and client identities outside the public chain. A new AGUS token adds liquidity, governance and legal exposure without solving a need that SOL for fees and stablecoins for settlement leave unmet. ## Lessons from hackathon winners

| Verified example | Possible economics and what the award establishes |
| --- | --- |
| Trepa, first in Breakout Consumer, and LootGo, Breakout Solana Mobile Award | A focused mobile prediction or discovery loop can differentiate. Partner placements or fee-funded events are possible if permitted. The awards verify judging results and reported prize amounts. They do not verify revenue or retention. [Breakout results](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-breakout-hackathon/) |
| Crypto Fantasy League, first in Breakout Gaming, and Supersize, first in Radar Gaming | A playable loop makes the chain's contribution visible. Cosmetics, sponsored tournaments or optional entry fees are hypotheses and can have consumer/gaming law constraints. The awards do not establish commercial margin. [Breakout results](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-breakout-hackathon/) · [Radar results](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-radar-hackathon/) |
| CargoBill, first in Breakout Stablecoin/Payments | A narrow, recognizable payment workflow can stand out. Per-invoice, integration and business settlement services are plausible. The award does not establish customer revenue. [Breakout results](https://blog.colosseum.com/announcing-the-winners-of-the-solana-breakout-hackathon/) |

Teams can win on execution, a clear problem, originality and a compelling demo before they have steady customers or a profitable business. Prize receipts are event awards. For AGUS the analogous demonstration is a real mobile payment with an invoice match and a verifiable explanation, followed by optional spending under a clearly shown policy.
