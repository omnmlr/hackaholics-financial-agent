# Business model and pricing hypotheses

**Keep the complete everyday multi-wallet workflow free to use.** It includes connecting and watching existing wallets, viewing supported balances and activity, a meaningful allowance for grounded questions and alerts, exact review and settlement verification. Ordinary external transfers carry applicable network costs. Using Pocket or an invoice is a user choice. Do not create a migration or transaction-volume requirement to get core value.

**Primary model · metered financial workflows and integrations.** Sell prepaid credits or pay-as-you-go access for costlier jobs that users request, such as extended historical backfills, verified export packs, high-frequency monitoring, approved batch operations, or structured event feeds. Individuals can buy a one-off pack. Teams and partners can pay per completed job, delivered event or incremental volume. Use a clear job price or allocation, show exactly what it delivers, and count successful delivery. Keep basic attribution, accuracy, ordinary monitoring, consent and recovery available independent of payment. Invoices and reconciliation exports can fit this model alongside account reports and portfolio exports.

**Secondary model · disclosed revenue from optional execution integrations.** Use partner-funded referrals where actual commercial terms and user value are verified, or an explicit AGUS fee on a supported route the user chooses. Never route to improve AGUS revenue at the user's expense. Provide a fair quote comparison with the standard route and show the provider/AGUS fee, minimum receive and network cost before approval. Direct transfers, Pocket funding or withdrawal and watch-only flows should have no AGUS percentage charge. Book no referral revenue before signed terms, operating approval and actual settlement.

## Illustrative monthly scenarios

Figures are planning hypotheses in USD. They assume direct use of external wallets and no recurring plan requirement. Delivery cost includes metered infrastructure and support allocations, and excludes acquisition, product engineering, tax and future regulated operations. The first three rows form one illustrative mix of distinct charges. The other rows are optional sensitivities that should not be added to the base.

| Model and payer | Illustrative usage and revenue | Main cost and dependency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow packs, paid by active users | 5,000 monthly active users × 4% redeeming a $6 pack = **$1,200**. | Paid depth must save enough time over free basic use. History replay, inference and support have measurable cost. |
| Team jobs, paid by small teams | 6 teams × 500 completed jobs × $0.15 = **$450**. | Durable schedules, permission visibility, instrumentation, audit history and per-action support. Charge for service/output, never for exercising an unverified grant. |
| Event/API access, paid by integration partners | 50,000 verified deliveries × $0.003 = **$150**. | Verified normalized events, retries, tenant isolation and a reliability commitment. Generic on-chain data alone is a commodity. |
| Optional swap fee, paid by user | $250,000 eligible monthly volume × a hypothetical 10 bps = **$250** before costs on an eligible route. Recognized base revenue **$0**. | Validate the actual endpoint and fee mint, assembly, landing, price impact and execution reliability first. Even $100 incremental delivery cost leaves a small $150 contribution under favorable fee assumptions. |
| On/off-ramp referral, paid by partner | **$0** in base. An illustrative $20,000 eligible volume at contracted 0.30% would be $60 before costs and credits. | Geography, KYC, partner terms and quote competitiveness. End-user ramp fees are separate. [MoonPay on-ramp](https://dev.moonpay.com/widget/on-ramp/overview) |
| Collection and invoicing, paid by request creator | **$0** in base. Test optional paid export or reconciliation jobs under the same credits if users ask for them. | A principal fee encourages bypass through direct transfers. Product viability must not depend on any invoice volume. |

The primary mix totals **$1,800**. An illustrative **$625** direct-cost envelope includes $49 indexed RPC, $151 database/workers/notifications, $150 inference, $225 support and $50 billing and delivery overhead. That leaves about **65% contribution before acquisition and engineering**. If only 2% redeem a pack and modeled team/API volume materializes, revenue is $1,200 and contribution is about 48% at the same cost. At 2% with no team/API uptake, $600 does not cover that envelope. A higher history ingestion rate, alert delivery or support load could require more capacity. Test cost per active wallet, return on monitoring, paid usage, route completion and gross contribution before promoting this to a forecast.

By the hackathon, measure product value and operational cost. There is no need to charge in the demo. Later, prove people return for reliable intelligence or monitoring independent of swap volume, then test paid jobs, actual delivery costs and partner economics. Review applicable regulation, custody, disclosure and data handling before charging for execution or conversion. Use SOL for network costs and stablecoins or conventional billing for service fees. An AGUS token adds no necessary utility to this model. ## Hackathon benchmark

Trepa won Breakout Consumer, LootGo won the Solana Mobile award, Crypto Fantasy League won Breakout Gaming, and CargoBill won Breakout Stablecoin/Payments. The organizer reports awards. They establish neither recurring revenue nor margins. The relevant standard for AGUS is a coherent mobile task that a judge can repeat with evidence, independent of billing.
